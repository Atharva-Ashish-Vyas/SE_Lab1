## Objective

This lab applies Agile Scrum practices in Jira by converting functional requirements into epics and user stories, prioritizing the backlog, estimating work with story points, simulating sprints, and analysing progress through burndown charts.

## Backlog Structure

The project backlog contains four epics and ten user stories derived from the functional requirements of the Telemedicine Slot Booking and Prescription Portal.

| Epic | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Doctor Discovery and Slot Booking | Helps patients discover doctors, view availability, and book consultation slots. |
| Payment and Booking Confirmation | Supports consultation payment and confirmation of successful bookings. |
| Video Consultation Delivery | Enables secure video consultation links and joining consultations. |
| Digital Prescription Management | Supports prescription authoring, validation, signing, and download. |

User stories were written in the Agile format:

> As a [user role], I want [goal], so that [benefit].

## Prioritisation and Estimation

All user stories were prioritised according to user impact and business value. High-priority stories supporting the core booking flow were completed first.

Story points were assigned using the Fibonacci scale: **2, 3, 5, and 8**. Estimates considered relative complexity, effort, risk, and uncertainty.

## Sprint Execution

| Sprint | Completed Stories | Story Points |
|---|---:|---:|
| TSBP Sprint 1 | 4 | 18 |
| TSBP Sprint 2 | 6 | 21 |
| **Total** | **10** | **39** |

Sprint 1 completed the core booking-flow stories, including doctor availability, appointment booking, payment, and secure video-link generation.

Sprint 2 completed the remaining consultation and prescription-related stories, including joining a consultation, prescription authoring and signing, payment failure handling, rescheduling, and prescription download.

All committed stories were moved through the Jira workflow:

`To Do -> In Progress -> Done`

Both sprints were completed with no rollover items.

## Burndown Analysis

The burndown charts for both sprints showed the remaining work reducing to zero by the end of each sprint.

- Sprint 1 began with approximately 18 story points.
- Sprint 2 began with approximately 21 story points.
- The work-progress line remained close to the ideal guideline.
- The project delivered an average velocity of approximately 19-20 story points per sprint.

## Reflection

### Did the estimations reflect the actual effort?

Yes. The burndown charts followed the expected trend, indicating that the assigned story points were reasonable. The prescription authoring and signing story was assigned 8 points because of its relatively higher complexity and uncertainty.

### Was the backlog well-prioritised?

Yes. The core user journey—discovering a doctor, checking availability, booking a slot, making payment, and receiving a video link—was prioritised in Sprint 1. The remaining features were completed in Sprint 2.

### How did the simulated sprint align with the plan?

Both sprints achieved 100% completion of committed stories. No scope was added during the sprints, and no stories were carried forward.

### What did the burndown chart reveal about team capacity?

The project completed 39 story points across two sprints, indicating an estimated capacity of around 19-20 story points per sprint. This can be used as a baseline for future sprint planning.
